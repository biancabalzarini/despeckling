This work explored the use of autoencoders for speckle noise reduction in SAR images, presenting original contributions in both the training data generation methodology and the development of flexible tools for experimenting with different architectures.

\section{Conclusions}

We have demonstrated that it is possible to use the $\mathcal{G}_I^0$ distribution to generate synthetic training data for training autoencoders capable of filtering speckle noise, although with limitations in their generalization to real images. This approach represents a novel contribution to the field and opens new lines of research on the generation of more sophisticated synthetic data.

Our experiments consistently reveal that deeper and more complex architectures yield better results, suggesting that the problem benefits from deep representations and that there is potential for improvement through the use of even more sophisticated architectures. The absence of overfitting in these complex architectures is an encouraging result, suggesting that the approach has significant room for improvement.

The comparison with traditional filtering methods validates our approach, showing that autoencoders can achieve comparable performance levels while offering advantages in terms of flexibility and optimization potential. However, the superior performance observed with models trained directly on real data underscores the importance of having training data that is representative of the application domain.

\section{Future Work}

The results of this work suggest several promising directions for future research:

\begin{itemize}
    \item Exploring deeper and more sophisticated architectures, including attention mechanisms and more elaborate U-Net-like architectures, could improve both noise reduction and detail preservation. The absence of overfitting in our experiments suggests that there is considerable room to increase model complexity.

    \item Developing more sophisticated synthetic data generation methods that better capture the complexity of real SAR images could close the observed generalization gap. This could include simulating more complex textures, incorporating realistic geometric features, and modeling specific artifacts from the SAR acquisition process.

    \item Investigating hybrid training schemes that combine pre-training with synthetic data followed by fine-tuning with real data could leverage the advantages of both approaches. This strategy could be particularly useful in situations where real data is scarce or expensive to obtain.

    \item Implementing and jointly applying the methodology proposed by Chan et al. \cite{chan2022entropy} and the approach developed in this work on the same set of images. This will allow for a more rigorous and equitable comparison between the two methods.

    \item Designing automatic and robust ways to measure the efficiency of models trained with real images is essential for objectively evaluating these filters. The development of such techniques would allow us to establish systematic comparisons between different approaches, thus facilitating the selection of the most appropriate model for specific applications.

    \item Finally, the extension of our work to polarimetric or multi-temporal SAR images represents a natural direction that could benefit from the ability of autoencoders to learn complex representations of multidimensional data.
\end{itemize}

In conclusion, this work establishes a solid foundation for the use of autoencoders in speckle noise reduction in SAR images and demonstrates the potential of combining statistical generative models with deep learning techniques to address classic problems in radar image processing.

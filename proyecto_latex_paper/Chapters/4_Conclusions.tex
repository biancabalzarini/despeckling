\section{Conclusions}
\label{chap:conclusions}

In this work, we explored the use of autoencoders for speckle noise reduction in SAR images, presenting original contributions in both the training data generation methodology and the development of flexible tools for experimenting with different architectures. We demonstrated that it is possible to use the $\mathcal{G}_I^0$ distribution to generate synthetic training data for training autoencoders capable of filtering speckle noise, although with limitations in their generalization to real images. Our experiments consistently reveal that deeper and more complex architectures yield better results. The comparison with traditional filtering methods validates our approach, showing that autoencoders can achieve comparable performance levels. However, the superior performance observed with models trained directly on real data underscores the importance of having training data that is representative of the application domain.

Our findings highlight a key dichotomy in the use of synthetic data: while the $\mathcal{G}_I^0$ model provides a statistically robust and controllable environment for initial architecture development and testing, a gap remains between synthetic and real-world data that hinders direct applicability. This suggests that the greatest potential for synthetic data may lie in hybrid training schemes, such as pre-training models before fine-tuning on smaller, real datasets. Furthermore, the consistent success of deeper models, coupled with a notable absence of overfitting, strongly indicates that the capacity of our models has not yet reached its upper limit.

Future research will focus on exploring more sophisticated architectures, such as U-Net and attention-based models, to enhance detail preservation. We also plan to refine synthetic data generation techniques and investigate hybrid training schemes that combine synthetic and real data to improve model generalization. A rigorous comparison with other state-of-the-art methods is also a priority. Finally, we will extend the proposed methodology to more complex data, including polarimetric and multi-temporal SAR images, to assess its performance in a broader range of applications. In conclusion, this work establishes a solid foundation for using autoencoders in speckle noise reduction in SAR images, demonstrating the potential of combining statistical models with deep learning to address classic radar image processing problems.
